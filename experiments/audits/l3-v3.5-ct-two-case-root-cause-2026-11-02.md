# L3 v3.5 CT comparison: root-cause follow-up (research only)

Date: 2026-11-02 (Japan). Status: **research candidate, NOT accepted for anatomical teaching**.

## Goal
Determine whether experimental inferior articular process extension after v3.3 addresses the lower-posterior reference-surface discrepancy. Protect v2.4 and v3.3 immutable snapshots.

## Grounded data
- Public VerSe20 whole-vertebra segmentation label L3=22, two different reference cases: verse005, verse008, SHA256 matched before each analysis.
- Synthetic and topology checks for v3.5 passed at both standard/high resolutions: one component, closed and manifold; inherited eight provisional dimensions within existing ±5% proxy thresholds.
- Geometries: v2.4 original, v3.3 posterior spinous extension, v3.5 v3.3 + inferior articular process extension.
- **All candidate evaluations are performed against the SAME registration based on v2.4**, rather than independently optimizing each shape.
- RAS-initialized rigid ICP (no scale), 6,000 random model/CT surface samples, near-surface distances calculated through nearest-neighbor lookup. Anatomical orientation was NOT verified by a qualified observer.

## Interpretation
1. The lower extension **reduces global CT-to-model P95 distance in both tested cases** in a fixed-registration test.
2. In some adjacent spatial bins P95 increases by 0.01–0.13 mm, so this is NOT a uniform improvement. Those changes are small and might be within sampling variability; do not treat their clinical significance as proven.
3. Geometric coverage and volume are not enough: surface normals, anatomical labels, articular geometry, and facet congruence remain unverified.
4. The CT masks are whole L3 only; the diagnostic bins are NOT individually segmented laminae, articular processes, or vertebral body. Do not attribute error causally to one structure solely from this proxy.

## Decision
**Hold v3.5 as a research candidate; keep v2.4 public and v3.3 available.** A qualified reference registration and anatomically labeled substructures are needed before accepting morphology or defining a quantitative final pass threshold.
